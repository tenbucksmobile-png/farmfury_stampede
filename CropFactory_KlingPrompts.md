> every asset: model **gpt-image2**, image-to-image, 2k / medium (15 credits a job), with **image 1 = a layout

> Three to five sprites per sheet (tiles / robots / pickups / obstacles+hazards / two scenery sheets), the boss alone
> at 1:1, its wreck image-to-image from the boss: 9 jobs for the whole world. A script then cuts the white out, removes
> the guide baselines and splits each sheet into trimmed sprites.

## 1. Getting the perspective right (read this first)

Kling's default is a "product shot": a 3/4 or isometric view with a shadow, because that is what most stock images of
"a crate" or "a robot" look like. A side-scrolling platformer needs a **flat side elevation**: the camera exactly level
with the object, looking straight at its side, with no top face visible. These help, roughly in order of how much:

1. **Start from an image, not just text (image-to-image / "reference image").** Text alone keeps drifting to 3/4.
   - For props and robots: use an existing in-game sprite that already has the right view as the reference
     (e.g. `Sprites/Robots/GlacierHarvestor_Right.png`, `Sprites/Robots/Robot_Harvest_right.png`,
     `Sprites/UI/MotherShip/PatrolRobot_right.png`) and describe only the *new* design. Keep reference strength
     medium-high so it copies the camera angle and outline weight, not the subject.
   - For tiles and the ground strip: sketch the shape yourself (even boxes in Paint/Photopea, side-on, flat) and use
     it as the reference. A 3-block flat rectangle is enough.
2. **Name the view the way game-art and technical drawings do.** Put this at the *front* of the prompt (Kling weights
   the start more):
   `2D side-scrolling game sprite, orthographic side elevation, camera level with the object, straight side profile,`
   Words that pull towards the wrong view and are best avoided: *block, cube, box, icon, asset pack, diorama, 3D,
   render, isometric, top* (even in "top surface") - say *"upper edge"* or *"walking surface along the top edge"*
   instead.
3. **Use the negative prompt box** (every prompt below):
   `isometric, 3/4 view, perspective, top view, bird's eye, visible top face, cube, 3D render, diorama, drop shadow,
   cast shadow, ground shadow, floor, text, watermark, logo, frame, border`
4. **One object per image.** Prompts asking for "a set of" or "a strip of 3" invite a top-down asset-sheet layout. For
   the ground, generate **one** block at a time and repeat it (or let me build the strip from one good block - the
   Sunken City and Mothership ground strips were generated by a script for exactly this reason).
5. **Pick the aspect ratio to suit the shape:** 16:9 for backgrounds, 1:1 for robots / props / single tiles, 16:9 or
   21:9 for long flat things (ledges, conveyor, laser beam). A square canvas for a long ledge makes Kling tilt it to
   fill the frame.
6. **Generate 4 and pick**, then re-run the winner with a lower variation / "similar" option rather than re-rolling
   the text.
7. **What I can fix afterwards, so don't fight Kling over it:** white backgrounds and soft shadows (cut out by script),
   facing left (mirrored), size and cropping, a slight tilt of a few degrees. What I can't fix: a visible top face or
   isometric angle - re-roll those.

### Style line - paste after every prompt

> *2D cartoon mobile game art, bold thick black outlines, flat cel shading with soft highlights, saturated colours,
> teal-grey industrial metal with warm red warning lights and yellow-black hazard stripes, chunky playful shapes,
> same style as a cartoon farm platformer, no text, no watermark*

### Cut-out line - add for robots, props, pickups, tiles

> *isolated on a plain pure white background, no shadow, no ground, flat side view, not isometric, not 3/4*

Kling can't output transparency; the white is removed by a script (as for Watermill Village). Robots and characters:
**facing right**, full body, feet touching the bottom edge of the image - the left-facing frames are mirrored unless
you'd rather draw them.

## 9. Robots

All facing right, full body, feet on the bottom edge, 1:1, with the cut-out line. Best results: image-to-image from an
existing robot sprite (`Sprites/Robots/GlacierHarvestor_Right.png`, `Robot_Harvest_right.png`,
`ScoutRobot_right.png`) so the side-on camera carries over, and the same seed / reference across all of them so they
read as one family.

**Harvester** (slow ground patrol)
```
2D side-scrolling game sprite, straight side profile facing right, a chunky cartoon harvester robot on caterpillar tracks, big rotating corn-reaping blades on its front, teal-grey riveted body, single angry red eye visor, hazard stripes, menacing but cute, full body
```

**Scout** (fast patrol)
```
2D side-scrolling game sprite, straight side profile facing right, a small fast cartoon robot on two springy legs with a bucket head full of carrots, single glowing red eye, antenna with a blinking light, teal-grey metal, running pose, full body
```

**Drone** (flyer)

```
2D side-scrolling game sprite, straight side profile facing right, a cartoon flying quadcopter robot with a small crop-sucking vacuum nozzle underneath, red eye, teal-grey body, spinning rotors
```

**Chaser** (optional - otherwise the shared Drift robot is used)
```
2D side-scrolling game sprite, straight side profile facing right, a lean cartoon robot on a single unicycle wheel leaning forward to chase, grabber claws out, red eye, teal-grey body, full body
```

**Boss: Harvester Prime** (the true final boss)
```
2D side-scrolling game sprite, straight side profile facing right, giant cartoon boss robot Harvester Prime: a huge combine-harvester body on tank treads, an enormous spinning reaper drum in front, two crusher-claw arms, glowing red eyes in a grinning steel face, gold trim and a crown-shaped exhaust stack puffing smoke, menacing and bold, full body
```

**Boss defeat (wreck)** - image-to-image from the chosen boss image
```
2D side-scrolling game sprite, straight side profile, the same giant combine-harvester boss robot knocked over and broken, lying on its back, dented, sparks and smoke, loose bolts and spilled corn cobs beside it
```
For the wreck, keep the body itself down at the bottom edge: loose debris below the body makes it look like it floats
(that happened with the Glacier Harvester's wreck).
